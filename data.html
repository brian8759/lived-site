Lived Data & Compliance

Effective and last updated September 21, 2026

Lived is operated by Baiqian Zhang. This is a plain-language overview of Lived’s current privacy, data, and platform-compliance design. It complements, but does not replace, the Privacy Policy.

Current production instrumentation

Current instrumentation systems

Apple App Store Connect AnalyticsApp Store acquisition, downloads, conversion, and Apple-side usage and retention information

Lived does not use Firebase Performance Monitoring. The current audited production archive does not link Apple’s AdSupport framework for analytics and does not use IDFA for advertising tracking.

Sensitive data boundary

Lived’s telemetry boundary is intended to block raw HealthKit measurements; photo contents, filenames, and PhotoKit identifiers; reflection and journal text; full receipt contents; free-form names, email addresses, and phone numbers; precise location; filesystem paths; arbitrary user-containing URLs; tokens; API keys; and secrets. Product events use coarse typed fields, such as result, workflow, receipt template, source, app version, and build number.

HealthKit, Photos, reflections, and receipts

Lived uses HealthKit only after permission to provide user-facing functionality. HealthKit data is not used for advertising, marketing, cross-app tracking, or sale, and raw HealthKit measurements are not intentionally sent to Firebase Analytics or Crashlytics. Lived may use Apple Photos APIs for photo-related functionality; photo contents and PhotoKit local identifiers are not intentionally placed in telemetry. Reflection text, journal content, and full receipt text are not Analytics parameters and are not deliberately attached to Crashlytics keys or logs.

Privacy controls and change management

Users control Health access in Apple’s Health/privacy settings, Photos access in iOS Settings, available Lived analytics and diagnostics controls, and locally stored content through app controls or removal. A privacy review is required when Lived materially changes data flows, including adding or changing analytics/diagnostics SDKs, advertising or attribution, accounts or cloud synchronization, a backend receiving personal content, or HealthKit/Photos use.

App Store privacy and service providers

Before every release, App Store privacy disclosures must be reconciled with the shipping binary, Xcode Organizer Privacy Report, exact Firebase SDK versions and enabled features, Firebase/Google Apple data-disclosure documentation, the privacy manifest, third-party SDK privacy manifests, Required Reason API declarations, and App Store Connect answers.

Relevant providers include Apple (iOS, HealthKit, Photos, MetricKit, App Store, and App Store Connect Analytics) and Google Firebase (Analytics and Crashlytics). Read Apple’s Privacy Policy, Firebase Privacy and Security, and Firebase’s Apple data-disclosure documentation.
